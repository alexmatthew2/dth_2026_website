## The Advantages of Open Source Software 

This is the first paragraph. The first paragraph after a headline is displayed in a lighter font weight.

The advantages of open source software are worth highlighting not just for tech enthusiasts, but for everyday users of digital technology. As technology becomes more and more integrated into our daily lives, it is important to be able to exercise some form of control over it, lest we cede our virtual spaces for work and creativity to corporations with profit-centered motivations. Open source software is ultimately about exercising true ownership over our own machines. 

What was the last frustration you had with a device you owned, where you felt as if you lacked a choice in the matter? Was it the inability to fully disable Microsoft Copilot from your computer? Bloatware on your phone such the game Angry Birds which, for some reason, you lacked permission to delete? The slowdown of an Apple product that you later found out was a deliberate design by the company to push you to buy a new device on a faster timeline (Gerken 2024)? All of these examples and more can be attributed to problems with closed software. Closed software can be compared to a house you bought, but without the master keys – all the modifications you make to it are ones that must be pre-approved by the former owner you bought it from, sometimes they will add new “features” overnight without you knowing, and there is a big box in the basement that somehow gets larger over time which you’re forever unable to unlock. Can such a house truly be yours if you lack the final say about how you want it to function? 

Having switched to Ubuntu Linux several years ago for the operating system on my laptop, I can state control over one’s own device is a significant quality-of-life improvement. I may not have the knowledge to regularly go in and modify the source code completely independently, but if I have a specific issue – such as wishing to delete a software program from my device completely, or encountering a known bug that the developers have not fixed yet – there is nothing preventing me from researching a solution online and attempting to implement it.  I can also customize my device to a greater degree than I could on the closed source operating systems Microsoft Windows and Apple macIOS. For instance, in 2017, the main fork of Ubuntu discontinued its use of the Unity graphical shell, whose look I preferred over its successor GNOME 3. Rather than resigning myself to the new look as would be necessary with any closed source software, I was able to switch to an Ubuntu fork which maintained Unity. 

While there are disadvantages to this level of control, as I could irreparably damage my device if I went into the terminal and started entering dangerous code with sudo permissions, oftentimes this is not a bad tradeoff. It is ultimately useful to have control over a countless number of micro-decisions, and in many matters in life, people prefer to have choices, even if it means they could make a bad one.
